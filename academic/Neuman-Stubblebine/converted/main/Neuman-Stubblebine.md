## 1  Introduction

This protocol defines a methodology for mutual authentication and key distribution within distributed systems.
It utilizes a nonce-based design to effectively mitigate the risks associated with replay attacks and reduces
the heavy reliance on globally synchronized clocks typically found in traditional protocols. The protocol is
characterized by a four-message initial authentication exchange and a highly efficient three-message subsequent
authentication process that does not require further contact with the authentication server.

## 2  Protocol Participants

The protocol involves three distinct entities:  
**A (Initiator):** The client or user who initiates the authentication request to access a service.  
**B (Responder):** The end-server or service provider that grants access to the initiator.  
**KDC (Key Distribution Center/K)**: The trusted third-party authentication server responsible for issuing credentials and session keys.

## 3  Notation

The following notation is used to describe the message exchanges :  
**A, B**: The unique identifiers (names) of Principal A and Principal B.  
**N_a, N_b**: Nonces (unique identifiers used only once) generated by A and B, respectively, to ensure message freshness.  
**K_ka, K_kb**: The long-term symmetric keys shared between the KDC and participants A and B, respectively.  
**K_ab**: The temporary session key generated by the KDC for secure communication between A and B.  
**T_b**: The expiration time for the credentials, defined relative to B's local clock and including an "epoch" identifier.  
**{M}\_K**: The encryption of message M using the symmetric key K.

## 4  A nonce-based protocol

let us consider a new protocol and some variations as
an exercise to better understand the benefits of the alternatives. This protocol is a nonce-based protocol
for repeated mutual authentication requiring only four messages initially and three messages for subsequent
authentication to a single server.

### 4.1  Initial authentication

(1) A → B: A, N_a  
(2) B → K: B, {A, N_a, T_b}K_kb, N_b  
(3) K → A: {B, N_a, K_ab, T_b}K_ka, {A, K_ab, T_b}K_kb, N_b  
(4) A → B: {A, K_ab, T_b}K_kb, {N_b}K_ab  
Our protocol begins with principal A initiating authentication by sending a cleartext message (1) to B
containing A's name, together with a nonce generated by A. B receives the message then sends message (2)
to the authentication server (K) containing B's name, instructions to the KDC to issue credentials to A,
and a nonce generated by B. These instructions specify A as the intended recipient of the credentials, a
suggested expiration time for the credentials, and the nonce received from A. The instructions are encrypted
in K_kb, the key registered for principal B with the KDC.  
Upon receiving instructions from B, K decrypts the instructions with K_kb, thus verifying that they
were issued by B at some point in the past (K does not care about replays). K then issues a ticket allowing
A to share a session key (K_ab) with B until the expiration time specified by B. In message (3), K sends
A, first, B's identity, the nonce originally generated by A, the session key K_ab and the expiration time
specified by B, all encrypted in K_ka, the key shared by the KDC and A; then the ticket; then B's nonce (N_b).  
Upon receiving message (3), A decrypts that part encrypted in K_ka and verifies that the nonce N_a is
the same as that included in message (1). If it matches A is assured that K received the nonce from B encrypted
in K_kb. Further, A knows B's nonce (N_b) and can use it to prove its identity to B. Finally, in message (4),
A forwards the ticket to B together with B's nonce (N_b) encrypted under the session key from the ticket, thus
proving to B the identity of A.

### 4.2  The ticket

The execution of the protocol leaves A in possession of a ticket that may be used for subsequent authentication
to B, avoiding the need to repeatedly contact the authentication server. The ticket specifies a session key to
be used between A and B (K_ab) and an indication of the time after which that key should no longer be
considered valid.  
The time in the ticket (in this case an expiration date) is relative to B's clock, and the time has been
generalized to include an epoch, an identifier for the current strand of time. If B's clock is set back,
the epoch is changed, and any tickets not issued in the current epoch are rejected.  
The current timestamp from B is not included. The expiration time is sufficient since the ticket can not be
used before it is issued.  
In this protocol, the ticket is issued by K and encrypted in the key shared by K and B, rather than issued
by B in a key known only to itself.

### 4.3  Subsequent authentication

At the end of the initial exchange, A is in possession of a ticket and session key that may be used to communicate with B.
A sends B a newly generated nonce (N_a'), with which B will authenticate itself to A, together with the ticket.
Upon receipt of the message, B decrypts the ticket, verifies that it has not expired, extracts the session key (K_ab),
then responds to A with a new nonce (N_b'), with which A will prove its identity to B, together with A's nonce
(N_a') encrypted in the session key (K_ab). A decrypts the message from B, verifies N_a'
and is assured of B's identity. A then encrypts N_b' in the session key and returns it to B, thus proving
its identity to B.

(1') A → B: N_a', {A, K_ab, T_b}K_kb  
(2') B → A: N_b', {N_a'}K_ab  
(3') A → B: {N_b'}K_ab

## 5  Assumptions

The Section 6 goals are evaluated only in the flow model's baseline world.
This analysis declares no additional world assumptions, no fixed object-level
assumptions, and no variable assumption dimensions. In particular, this
document does not define a long-term-key disclosure or old-session-key
exposure scenario for this protocol.

## 6  Security Properties

Exactly four authentication goals are posed in the baseline scenario.

- **B's injective agreement with A.** `B` is the asserter and `A` is the
  subject of injective agreement on `(A, B, K_ab, N_a, N_b)`.
- **B's non-injective agreement with A.** `B` is the asserter and `A` is the
  subject of non-injective agreement on `(A, B, K_ab, N_a, N_b)`.
- **A's injective agreement with B.** `A` is the asserter and `B` is the
  subject of injective agreement on `(A, B, N_a, N_b, T_b)`.
- **A's non-injective agreement with B.** `A` is the asserter and `B` is the
  subject of non-injective agreement on `(A, B, N_a, N_b, T_b)`.

All four goals are evaluated only in the baseline. No session-key secrecy goal
is part of this analysis.
