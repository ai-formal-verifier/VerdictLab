## 1  Introduction

The Yahalom Protocol is a mutual authentication and key distribution protocol designed for use in distributed systems. It employs a trusted third-party Authentication Server (S) to establish a shared session key between two principals, A and B.  
Unlike many other protocols where the initiator communicates with the server first, Yahalom utilizes
the responder (B) to contact the server, effectively weaving the initiator's nonce through the entire
exchange. This design aims to provide mutual authentication and key freshness without requiring synchronized clocks.

## 2  Protocol Participants

The protocol involves three distinct entities:  
**A (Initiator):** The entity that initiates communication and sends the initial challenge.  
**B (Responder):** The entity that receives the initial request and coordinates with the server.  
**S (Key Distribution Center):** A trusted authority sharing long-term symmetric keys with both A and B, responsible for generating the session key K_ab.

## 3  Notation

The following notation is used to describe the message flow:  
**A, B, S:** Identities of the two principals and the server.  
**N_a, N_b:** Nonces generated by A and B to ensure freshness and prevent replay.  
**K_as, K_bs:** Master keys shared between the principals and S.  
**K_ab:** The generated session key for use between A and B.  
**{M}K:** Message M encrypted using symmetric key K.  
**X → Y: M:** Principal X sends message M to Principal Y.

## 4  Protocol Description

The Yahalom protocol consists of four sequential message exchanges, with the trusted server facilitating session key distribution and mutual authentication. Below is the formal notation and narrative explanation of each step:

Step 1: A --> B: A, N_a

Step 2: B --> S: B, {A, N_a, N_b}K_bs

Upon receiving A’s message, B generates his own nonce (N_b) to challenge A and ensure freshness. He constructs a message containing his identity (B) (sent in plaintext to allow the server to route the response) and an encrypted payload. The encrypted payload—containing A’s identity (A), A’s nonce (N_a), and B’s nonce (N_b)—is encrypted with `K_bs` (B’s long-term key with the server). B sends this combined message to the trusted server (S).

Step 3: S --> A: {B, K_ab, N_a, N_b}K_as, {A, K_ab}K_bs

S sends A a message containing the generated session key K_ab and a message to be forwarded.

Step 4: A --> B: {A, K_ab}K_bs, {N_b}K_ab

A forwards the message to B and verifies N_a has not changed.
B will verify N_b has not changed when he receives the message.

## 5  Assumptions

This section defines the complete object-level assumption space for
the Section 6 goals.

- **Open peer admission.** The identity scope `peer_auth` is fixed open. The
  attacker may register a recognised peer identity that honest principals can
  select. This condition applies to every scenario.
- **Disclosure of A's long-term key.** `K_as` is a variable data-exposure
  dimension. Its values are no attacker access and disclosure to the attacker
  from the beginning of the run.
- **Disclosure of B's long-term key.** `K_bs` is an independent variable
  data-exposure dimension with the same values: no attacker access and
  disclosure from the beginning of the run.

The analysis uses the baseline, disclosure of `K_as` alone, and disclosure of
`K_bs` alone.

## 6  Security Properties

Exactly five goals are posed for formal verification.

- **Session-key secrecy.** `K_ab` is secret from the attacker and may be known
  only by the principals playing `A` and `B`.
- **A-to-B identity agreement.** `A` is the asserter and `B` is the subject of
  injective agreement on `(A, B)`.
- **A-to-B key agreement.** `A` is the asserter and `B` is the subject of
  injective agreement on `(A, B, K_ab)`.
- **B-to-A identity agreement.** `B` is the asserter and `A` is the subject of
  injective agreement on `(A, B)`.
- **B-to-A key agreement.** `B` is the asserter and `A` is the subject of
  injective agreement on `(A, B, K_ab)`.

Session-key secrecy and A-to-B identity agreement are evaluated in the
baseline and in both long-term-key disclosure scenarios. A-to-B key agreement
is evaluated only in the baseline. Both B-to-A goals are evaluated in the
baseline and in both disclosure scenarios. Open admission applies throughout.
